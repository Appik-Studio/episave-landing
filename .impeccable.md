## Design Context

### Users
Mixed audience in roughly equal measure:
- **Patients & caregivers** — people living with epilepsy and their families seeking safety solutions
- **Medical professionals** — neurologists, researchers, and clinicians evaluating the device
- **Donors & partners** — NGOs, foundations, and individuals considering funding or collaboration

Each group needs to quickly find credibility signals: clinical rigor for doctors, emotional reassurance for caregivers, and impact evidence for donors.

### Brand Personality
**Clinical, precise, credible.**

The site should feel like a serious medical-tech product, not a startup pitch or charity appeal. Think Withings or Oura — clean, premium, and quietly authoritative. Every design choice should reinforce that this is a well-engineered, trustworthy medical device.

**Target emotion**: Confidence & trust. Visitors should feel: "This is a serious, well-built medical solution I can rely on."

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone**: Clean medical-tech. Premium and restrained, not warm or playful.
- **References**: Withings, Oura — minimal layouts, generous whitespace, precise typography, muted color palette with selective accent use.
- **Anti-references**: Charity/nonprofit aesthetics (emotional imagery, bright call-to-action colors), consumer tech hype (gradients, glassmorphism, excessive animation).
- **Theme**: Light mode primary (dark mode tokens exist but theme toggle is disabled).
- **Palette direction**: The current warm purple & amber palette is **open to reconsideration** — a cooler, more clinical palette may better serve the credible/precise positioning. Consider muted blues, slate grays, and restrained accent colors aligned with medical-tech conventions.
- **Current typography**: IBM Plex Sans (400-700 + italics) via Google Fonts — single typeface for institutional clinical precision.
- **Iconography**: Carbon Design System icons (inline SVG, 24x24).

### Design Principles

1. **Clinical credibility over warmth** — Every visual choice should reinforce medical-grade trust. Prefer restrained color use, precise alignment, and generous whitespace over decorative elements.

2. **Information density matched to audience** — Stats, features, and evidence should be immediately scannable. Doctors, caregivers, and donors all need to find relevant proof points fast.

3. **Quiet confidence** — Let the product speak through clarity, not flashiness. Subtle animations (respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`), clean transitions, and unhurried pacing.

4. **Accessible by default** — WCAG AA compliance as baseline. Given the epilepsy context, be mindful of motion even though AAA/reduced-motion isn't formally required — avoid rapid flashing or strobing effects entirely.

5. **Bilingual parity** — EN and FR must feel equally polished. Design decisions (spacing, line lengths, button sizes) must accommodate French text expansion (~15-20% longer than English).
